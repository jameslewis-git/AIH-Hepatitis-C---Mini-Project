🎯 Clinical Problem & Project Motivation

Why Machine Learning for Hepatitis C Staging?

Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) affects over 58 million people globally. It is commonly dubbed the "silent killer" because infected patients remain asymptomatic for decades while active viral replication slowly destroys functional liver parenchyma.

⚠️ The Diagnostic Dilemma:
  • Needle Liver Biopsy: Invasive, painful, carries bleeding and puncture risks (1 in 500), and costs $1,200–$2,500.
  • Transient Elastography (FibroScan): Non-invasive but requires specialized $50,000+ hardware unavailable in primary care or rural facilities.
💡 The Machine Learning Diagnostic Solution:

Standard Liver Function Tests (LFT) cost only $15–$30 and are available in any diagnostic pathology lab. By utilizing advanced machine learning on 12 routinely collected biochemical markers, this clinical model stages hepatic progression with {{ accuracy }} accuracy non-invasively.

🎯 Why Macro-F1 was the Selection Criterion

A naive model that predicts "Healthy Blood Donor" on every sample achieves 86.7% raw accuracy while having zero diagnostic utility for hepatitis or cirrhosis! To ensure patient safety, our training pipeline chooses the model with the highest Macro-F1 score, which computes unweighted average F1 across all 5 classes equally.

🩺 Clinical Insight: The De Ritis Ratio (AST / ALT Inversion)

In early or acute hepatitis, ALT > AST because ALT is exclusively cytoplasmic. However, as hepatitis progresses to Fibrosis and Cirrhosis, deep mitochondrial lysis and impaired sinusoidal clearance cause AST to exceed ALT (De Ritis Ratio > 1.0). The model automatically discovered this landmark clinical principle, making AST (21.9%) and synthetic enzyme CHE (19.3%) its two most decisive attributes!

🎓 Mini-Project Presentation & Viva Defense

Frequently Asked Viva Evaluation Questions

Key questions professors and project reviewers commonly ask, with authoritative machine learning defenses.
✓ Presentation Defense Ready
Q1. Why use XGBoost/Random Forest instead of a Deep Neural Network?
Defense: The dataset has 615 tabular rows. Deep Neural Networks require tens of thousands of samples to learn representations and suffer severely from overfitting on small clinical tables. Gradient Boosted Trees consistently outperform deep learning on tabular data while offering native feature importance rankings.
Q2. How did you ensure there is no Data Leakage in missing value imputation?
Defense: We strictly performed imputer.fit(X_train) on the training fold only, and subsequently transformed X_test and live inputs using those fixed median values. Computing medians across the entire dataset before splitting would cause test-set distribution leakage.
Q3. Why is AST more important than ALT in your feature ranking?
Defense: While ALT is liver-specific for acute hepatitis, AST exists in both mitochondria and cytoplasm. When disease advances from hepatitis to fibrosis and cirrhosis, mitochondrial disruption causes AST to outpace ALT (Inverted De Ritis Ratio: AST/ALT > 1.0). The model successfully learned this biological hallmark.
Q4. How did you handle the extreme 86.7% class imbalance?
Defense: We used Stratified Splitting (guaranteeing exact class ratios in test sets), Balanced Class Weighting (penalizing minority class errors inversely proportional to frequencies), and Macro-F1 selection to prevent the model from defaulting to the 86.7% majority.
Q5. What if a patient arrives with only 3 or 4 blood markers?
Defense: The production API seamlessly imputes unprovided markers using the training median baseline. The system explicitly discloses which markers were estimated vs measured in the UI flags, ensuring clinical transparency without crashing.
Q6. What is the role of the Vision OCR and Symptom Screener?
Defense: They bridge the gap between AI and clinical workflow: Gemini 1.5 Flash Vision OCR allows digitizing physical lab printouts directly without typing, while the Symptom Screener enables preliminary risk stratification when lab tests are unavailable.
